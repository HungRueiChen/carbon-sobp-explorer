Model assumptions
  • Toy model for teaching, not a treatment planning calculation. Absolute values are illustrative; the directions of change are the point.
  • Pristine peaks: Bragg–Kleeman stopping power S ∝ (R−z)^(1/p−1), p = 1.77, Gaussian range straggling widened by a ripple filter (σ ≈ 0.2 cm), primary fluence attenuated as exp(−z/28 cm).
  • Fragments: dose builds up with depth as primaries are lost and continues past each peak as an exponential tail. Fragment LET is fixed at 12 keV/µm.
  • Primary LET follows the same power law: about 13 keV/µm at 16 cm residual range, rising to well over 100 keV/µm in the last millimetre.
  • Fluence: primary carbon ions only. Each beam's fluence is set by its weight through Φ = D / (1.602×10⁻⁹ · LET) (Gy, keV/µm, ions/cm², water), falls by nuclear attenuation with depth, and drops to zero across its straggled range (error-function edge). Fragment fluence is not plotted, although fragment dose and LET are included in the dose and LETd curves.
  • Biology: HSG-like reference cells, photon α = 0.313 Gy⁻¹, β = 0.0615 Gy⁻². Carbon α rises with dose-averaged LET above 8 keV/µm with an overkill roll-off; β is held equal to the photon value (mixed-beam rule).
  • RBE is solved from LQ isoeffect at the local physical dose, so it also depends on the prescription per fraction.
  • Peak weights are a non-negative, lightly smoothed least-squares fit to the physical dose each depth needs. The fit and the LET-dependent RBE are iterated until RBE × physical dose is flat across the SOBP. The default 3.6 Gy(RBE) per fraction matches a common 57.6 Gy(RBE)/16 fraction prostate schedule.
